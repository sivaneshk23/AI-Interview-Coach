# Interview Fundamentals

## Technical Interviews

Technical interviews evaluate whether a candidate understands the concepts,
tools, algorithms, technologies, and practical techniques required for a
target job role.

A strong technical answer should normally contain:

1. A clear definition or explanation.
2. The relevant technical concept.
3. A practical example when appropriate.
4. Important advantages, limitations, or trade-offs.
5. The reasoning behind the proposed solution.

Candidates should avoid memorized answers that do not demonstrate actual
understanding.

---

## Behavioral Interviews

Behavioral interviews evaluate how candidates have handled situations,
problems, teamwork, leadership, conflict, failure, responsibility, and
decision-making.

A useful structure for behavioral answers is STAR:

- Situation — Explain the context.
- Task — Explain the responsibility or objective.
- Action — Explain what the candidate actually did.
- Result — Explain the outcome.

Candidates should focus on their own actions rather than describing only
what the team did.

---

## HR Interviews

HR interviews commonly assess:

- Motivation
- Career goals
- Communication
- Professionalism
- Teamwork
- Adaptability
- Strengths
- Weaknesses
- Work preferences
- Organizational fit

Answers should be honest, concise, professional, and relevant to the
question.

---

## Communication

Good interview communication should be:

- Clear
- Structured
- Concise
- Relevant
- Professional

Candidates should avoid unnecessarily complicated language.

A technically correct answer can still be weak if the explanation is
unclear or does not directly answer the question.

---

## Technical Answer Evaluation

Technical correctness should be evaluated separately from communication.

An answer may be:

- technically correct but poorly communicated,
- well communicated but technically incorrect,
- partially correct,
- incomplete,
- irrelevant,
- or incorrect.

The evaluator should distinguish these dimensions rather than assigning a
score based only on confidence or writing quality.

---

## Adaptive Interviewing

An adaptive interview changes subsequent questions based on candidate
performance.

If a candidate demonstrates strong understanding, the interviewer can
increase difficulty or introduce deeper concepts.

If a candidate demonstrates weak understanding, the interviewer can ask a
simpler follow-up question or test the underlying concept.

The system should avoid unnecessarily repeating questions.

---

## Personalized Training

A useful interview trainer should identify recurring weaknesses.

Examples include:

- SQL knowledge gaps
- Python fundamentals
- Data structures
- Algorithms
- Statistics
- Machine learning concepts
- Communication problems
- Incomplete explanations
- Weak behavioral examples
- Difficulty explaining projects

The training system can use these weaknesses to recommend targeted practice.

---

## Fair Evaluation

The interview evaluator should evaluate the content of the candidate's
answer rather than making judgments based on protected personal
characteristics.

Evaluation should focus on:

- correctness,
- relevance,
- completeness,
- clarity,
- communication,
- reasoning,
- and role-specific competency.
